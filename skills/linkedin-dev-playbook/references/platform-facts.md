## Feed ranking: published versus practitioner

**[LinkedIn]**

- 2026 rebuild: one LLM-embedding retrieval system replaced network, trending and topic sources; a sequential "Generative Recommender" ranks with 1,000+ past interactions. Targets: click, skip, long dwell, like, comment, share. The post's representation includes author name, headline, company, industry, format, text and engagement percentiles; embeddings refresh within minutes (Engineering blog, March 2026).
- Dwell starts when half the update is visible; a predicted skip lowers the score (Engineering blog, 2020).
- March 2026 feed update (Tim Jurka): filter engagement bait, make pods ineffective, curb comment automation through third-party software or extensions, show less video unrelated to its text and less recycled thought leadership, more actionable insight.
- Comment quality is judged from the comment, not from likes on it (LinkedIn corporate communications, 2026, quoted in press; unnamed spokesperson, so treat as [LinkedIn, via press]).
- Links: "We don't intentionally limit the reach of posts with links" (Rishi Jobanputra, LinkedIn Sr. Director of Product Management, video Q&A around September 2025, relayed by Matt Navarra on Threads) [LinkedIn, via practitioner relay]. Hashtags: "a nice to have, not a need to have" (LinkedIn staff in 2025 interviews relayed by Buffer and ZoomSphere) [LinkedIn, via practitioner relay].
- AI content: allowed if it reflects the member's voice; generic content distributed less beyond the network; readers can mark "Seems like AI slop", which shows the author a tip in post analytics (Help a1481496, Pressroom 2026).
- 360Brew: a research pre-production model (arXiv 2501.16450); Tim Jurka (LinkedIn VP Engineering) said on 14 April 2026 that it does not run the feed, quoted by liamhayward.co.uk and espirian.co.uk [LinkedIn, via practitioner relay].
- Comment pinning: authors can pin helpful comments on their posts (Pressroom, news.linkedin.com/2026/new-ways-to-build-meaningful-connections).

**[practitioner]**

- Link in post body: AuthoredUp -40 to -50% on profiles; van der Blom -18.8% (his 2025 edition said +5%); Ordinal -26.5% (sells link-in-comment automation); Saywhat positive. A "60% penalty" has no traceable source. The likeliest mechanism is attention leaving the post.
- First 60 to 90 minutes decide distribution: van der Blom, AuthoredUp, PostHog handbook. LinkedIn confirms fast refresh, not a fixed window.
- Median impressions: 1,006 per personal post versus 659 per Page post (AuthoredUp, September 2026); organic company content about 1-2% of feeds (van der Blom).
- Format reach on profiles: polls 1.64x, documents 1.45x, images 1.18x, video 1.10x, text 0.88x; on Pages documents 1.40x, text 0.42x (AuthoredUp).
- Hashtags: no measured effect; six or more hurt (AuthoredUp).
- Saves as the top signal, a "Depth Score", or edits killing reach: no LinkedIn source.

## Pages, employees and advocacy

- Pages represent organizations; a personal profile used for a company can be restricted (a6204405).
- Admins comment and react as the Page, repost staff posts as the Page, and reshare @mentions from the Activity tab (a700698, a551424).
- Notify employees reaches a relevance-picked subset of associated employees (a549372). Association with the Page takes up to 30 days (a542848).
- Thought Leader Ads sponsor an employee's post from the Page; LinkedIn's 2023 pilot reported 1.7x CTR, labelled directional.
- Page verification ties to a commercial relationship: Ads, Jobs or a Premium Page (a6275638). Verifying the Page and members' workplaces helps posting and comment capacity and visibility (a528176, a700698).
- Pages weekly posting: "Companies that post weekly see 2x higher engagement" (business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/linkedin-pages, LinkedIn data, April 2025); comparator unstated, correlational, no method published.
- After the Employee Advocacy tab, LinkedIn points admins to LinkedIn Marketing Partners for advocacy tools (a6836874).

## Removed or changed features

- My Company tab, Employee Advocacy tab and curator role: discontinued from November 2024 (a6836874). No native replacement.
- Creator Mode toggle: removed in 2024, its tools opened to everyone [practitioner: Taplio, Bluecast]; "make follow primary" remains [LinkedIn] (a528011).
- LinkedIn Live: unscheduled go-live ended 22 June 2026; an Event can still be scheduled minutes ahead [LinkedIn, via press] (Social Media Today, 29 March 2026).
- Page follow-invite credits: number withdrawn from Help; reported 250 to 50 in 2026.
- Newsletter creation: open to all members (older guides cite a follower threshold for individuals).

## Measurement definitions

- Member post analytics: impressions (estimate, in- and out-of-network split), members reached (estimate), link visits including repeats, profile viewers and followers gained (a516971, a701208). Viewer demographics (job title, seniority, company size, company) appear only when enough unique viewers exist and never on connections-only posts (a516971). No member engagement rate.
- Page analytics: an impression counts at 50% on screen for 300 ms; engagement rate = (clicks + reactions + comments + shares) / impressions; CTR = clicks / impressions; metrics can lag 48 hours (a564051).
- Socialinsider's Page benchmark (5.20% average in 2025; documents highest, link posts lowest at 2.50% in Q3 2026) excludes clicks, so it is not the same metric [practitioner].
- LinkedIn shortens long links; check that UTM parameters survive before trusting referral data [practitioner].

## Disclosure law

- US: an employee promoting the employer's product discloses the relationship; an employer that directs such posts trains and monitors them; incentives such as points or prizes are disclosed too (FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR 255.5, examples 8 and 9). On social media the disclosure should be unavoidable, inside the post (16 CFR 255.0) [law].
- UK: content from people connected to a brand, employees included, must be obviously identifiable as advertising; labels hidden behind "see more" or after hashtags fail, and a bio or earlier posts are not enough (ASA/CAP, recognising ads on social media) [law].
- Neither is legal advice; send programs with incentives to counsel.
