## The one question that decides if it's worth $15k

DevRelCon's audience is **DevRel and developer-marketing practitioners** — not, by default, the engineers who evaluate and buy CI/CD tooling. Some overlap exists (DevRel folks often have real influence on their own org's tool choices, and Ambervale selling *to* companies with DevRel teams is a legitimate niche), but it's not the same room as a DevOps/platform-engineering audience. Before spending, decide honestly which outcome you're buying:

- **Employer brand / practitioner network / recruiting** — valid goal for a DevRel budget line, but judge it on relationships and visibility among peers, not pipeline.
- **Direct CI/CD pipeline** — only works if a meaningful slice of attendees are actual buyers or technical influencers. Ask Hoopy for the role breakdown before you commit; if it's <15-20% engineering/ops roles, this isn't a pipeline play, and $15k + booth should be sized like a brand/community spend, not a sales spend.

Do the math once you have real numbers: (sponsorship + travel + staff time + booth build) ÷ realistic qualified conversations = cost per qualified meeting. Compare that to what a meeting costs you from outbound or ads today. If DevRelCon can't beat that without "but it's good for our brand" doing all the work, that's your answer.

## What to negotiate with Hoopy, in this order

1. A **speaking or panel slot** — converts better than booth space, and often gets thrown in or discounted for a sponsor.
2. **Side-event rights** — permission to run a dinner or breakfast for a curated list during the conference. This is usually the highest-ROI line item you can get.
3. **A real attendee list or opt-in mechanism** — get it in writing, not "impressions."
4. **Booth placement** — near coffee/entrance/main-stage exit, not a back corner.
5. **Price last**, once the package is actually right.

Given a 30-person company, seriously consider **skipping the full booth and running a curated dinner instead** (8-14 people, hand-picked DevRel leads at companies that could plausibly buy or influence CI/CD purchases, or that make good referral partners). It costs a fraction of a booth, produces deeper conversations, and you don't need Hoopy's permission to do it adjacent to the conference.
