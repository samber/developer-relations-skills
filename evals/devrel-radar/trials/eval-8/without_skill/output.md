# Hollick Systems ramp-up: 4 weeks, 2h/week, before 2026-10-14

You're not short on technical depth — you're short on practice in the two skills this role actually runs on: writing/speaking for an external audience, and packaging technical truth for sales/CS. That's what this plan closes. Events and travel are out of scope on purpose — no budget until next fiscal year, so everything below is remote-native.

One framing to sit with all month: **advocacy and enablement pull in opposite directions when you're the only owner.** Advocacy wants you writing publicly and building community trust; enablement wants you in internal Slack threads unblocking a sales engineer's demo by Thursday. Nobody can tell you the right split before you've seen the backlog — so don't try to master both. Spend this month getting fluent in the *inputs* to each, and leave the prioritization question for your manager on day one.

## Week 1 — Frame the job (2h)

- Read up on the advocacy/enablement split as a discipline, not just a title. Look for Mary Thengvall's writing on DevRel's business value, and the DevRel Collective's public materials — both spell out why "advocacy" (external trust, content, community) and "enablement" (internal technical enablement of sales/CS/partners) are treated as separate functions even when one person owns both.
- Research Hollick Systems like a prospective user, not an employee: product, docs site, changelog, GitHub org (issues, discussions, contributing guide), any existing blog/YouTube/newsletter, Discord/Slack community if one exists.
- Find out who did advocacy/enablement at Hollick before you (predecessor, a founder doing it part-time, nobody). Their leftover artifacts — old blog posts, a stale battlecard, a dead Slack channel — tell you more about real expectations than the job post did.
- Note names: your manager, the sales engineers or CS leads you'll likely support, and anyone technical who's publicly active (GitHub, Twitter/LinkedIn) under the Hollick name.

## Week 2 — Use the product like a brand-new user (2h)

- Sign up and run the quickstart cold, with your platform-engineer instincts turned toward "where would *I* get stuck," not "where's this well-engineered." Write down every friction point — this list becomes your first credible contribution.
- Read the last ~20 GitHub issues, forum posts, or support-adjacent questions you can find. Look for the 2-3 questions that repeat — that's the actual enablement gap, regardless of what the docs claim to cover.
- If Hollick has a Discord/Slack/forum, join and lurk. Note tone, who answers questions, how fast, and whether anyone is already doing informal advocacy.

## Week 3 — Build the muscles that are actually new to you (2h)

- Draft one short technical post (600-800 words) about something you hit in week 2 — a rough edge, a "here's how X actually works" explainer. Don't aim to publish yet; aim to feel what "write for a stranger, not a teammate" costs you. This is the single highest-leverage rep you can get before day one.
- Find 2-3 people doing advocacy well in a developer-tool category adjacent to Hollick's. Study their format choices (long-form vs. short video vs. code-first threads), not just their topics — you're reverse-engineering a cadence you can sustain solo.
- Skim what a sales one-pager, a battlecard, and a demo script look like (search "developer tool battlecard example" or similar). You don't need to write one yet — you need to recognize the shape so week-one requests don't blindside you.

## Week 4 — Get day-one-ready (2h)

- Draft a rough 30/60/90 outline for your first 1:1 — not a finished plan, a set of questions: what's the current advocacy/enablement split in practice, what metrics does Hollick already track (GitHub stars, docs traffic, community activity, sales-assist requests), who owns the blog and community platforms today, what's the single most annoying recurring ask from sales or support.
- Polish the two profiles people will actually check before trusting you publicly: LinkedIn headline/about, GitHub profile readme and pinned repos.
- Subscribe to 2-3 DevRel-specific newsletters or blogs (search "DevRel newsletter" — Developer Relations Weekly and similar exist) so learning continues past this month without more scheduled prep time.
- Confirm with your manager, before day one if you can, when the "next fiscal year" actually starts — it changes how soon travel/events become live options, and whether it's worth prepping a conference CFP pitch in month two or three.

## What to explicitly *not* do this month

- Don't build a content calendar or KPI framework yet — you don't have Hollick's real numbers or backlog, and a plan built on guesses gets thrown out in week one anyway.
- Don't over-invest in event/conference prep — no budget until next fiscal year, so it's dead weight against your 8 available hours.
- Don't try to become equally strong at advocacy and enablement in one month. Get conversant in both; let day-one conversations tell you which one needs you first.
